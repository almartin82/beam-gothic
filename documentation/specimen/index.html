BEAM GOTHIC

A caps-only display face drawn after the lettering of the Bethlehem Steel wordmark, as it ran on catalog covers from 1931 to 1966. The wordmark only ever spelled two words, so the archive holds seven letters. Everything else was cut by the rules those seven follow.

The cutting rules

Three rules were read off the seven documented letters. A fourth was added for the accents, which the source never needed.

H

1. Sheared stem ends

Every free stem end is cut on the same rising diagonal, slope 0.45, low point at the left. Ends that join another stroke stay square. Read from the T, H and M.

E

2. Spurred arms

On a free arm end the outer edge runs 56 units longer than the inner. Top arms point up and feet point down. Read from the E and L.

O

3. No curves

Bowls and rounds are rectangles with clipped corners, 60 units outside and 22 inside. Read from the B and S, and carried through the O, the figures and the ring accent.

ÉÅ

4. Accents: drawn once, kept low

Each accent is cut in the same language: the acute and grave are sheared slabs, the dieresis is two small periods, the circumflex and caron are chevrons, the tilde a zigzag, the ring a clipped square. They sit 45 units above the cap line and stay wide and flat, because letters this heavy can carry a low accent and a tall one would force the lines apart. Every accented letter is composed from a letter and its accent, never redrawn.
