BEAM GOTHIC

A caps-only display font, drawn from the lettering in the old Bethlehem Steel wordmark. The wordmark only gives you seven letters. I drew the rest from the rules those seven follow.

In the original wordmarkDrawn by the rules
BURNS HARBOR
PLANT · INDIANA

Bethlehem's mill on the Indiana side of Lake Michigan. They never drew a U, N, R, A or O in this style, so most of this line is my guess.

Where it started

I took this picture at a Bills game on November 22, 2026. The banner hangs in the Blue Moon Bar at Highmark Stadium (Oxford Pennant, out of Buffalo, made it) and I wanted to know what font it was. It isn't one. The letters are hand-cut felt, copied from the mark Bethlehem Steel put on its own catalogs. So I went looking for the catalogs.

Character set

344 glyphs, which covers Google Fonts Latin Core - accented capitals for most of Europe, with the lowercase keys typing the same capitals. The seven red letters are the ones in the catalogs. Everything in black is mine.

The cutting rules

Three rules come straight off the seven original letters. I added a fourth for the accents, since Bethlehem never needed one.

H

1. Sheared stem ends

Where a stem ends in open space, it gets cut on a rising diagonal, low on the left. Where it joins another stroke it stays square. You can see it on the T, H and M.

E

2. Spurred arms

Where an arm ends in open space, its outside edge runs a little longer than the inside (56 units). Top arms kick up, feet kick down. That's from the E and L.

O

3. No curves

Anything that would be round is a rectangle with the corners clipped off. The B and S work that way in the original, so the O, the numbers and even the ring accent do too.

ÉÅ

4. Accents, kept low

The accents are cut the same way as the letters: slabs for the acute and grave, two small periods for the dieresis, chevrons for the circumflex and caron, a zigzag for the tilde. I kept them low and wide. I think letters this heavy can carry that, and tall accents would have pushed the lines apart.
